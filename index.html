Motivation
Why CERN

My interest in CERN's Vacuum, Surfaces and Coatings group stems from a genuine intellectual connection between my current work and the fundamental questions your group addresses. During my internship at ONGC — India's largest oil and gas corporation — I have been working hands-on with protective coating systems and pipeline integrity assessment using Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) and Cathodic Protection monitoring. This experience has given me a practical grounding in how coating performance degrades under real industrial conditions, and has made me deeply curious about a question I cannot answer in that setting: what happens to coating behaviour, outgassing characteristics, and surface interactions when these systems are pushed into the ultra-high vacuum regime?

That question points directly to CERN's TE-VSC group. The work on NEG coatings, thin-film deposition, and electrochemical surface treatments for accelerator hardware represents the frontier of exactly this problem — surfaces performing at the intersection of extreme vacuum, radiation, and cryogenic conditions. My background in computational materials science (DFT, molecular dynamics, COSMO-RS) gives me the ability to think about surface-gas interactions at the molecular level, and my hands-on corrosion and coating work at ONGC is building the experimental intuition I need to make that computational knowledge useful in a laboratory setting.

With LS3 now underway and the HL-LHC upgrade in full preparation, I understand this is one of the most active periods in TE-VSC's history. Contributing to that work — even in a supporting capacity — would be a formative experience I am deeply motivated to pursue.
